# Raw argument transcript - Week 6 TECHNOLOGIST

**Yonathan:** Week 6. I am the TECHNOLOGIST. Let's begin. My first idea is obvious: a VR earthquake simulator for Mexican schools.

**Model:** That is a technology category, not yet a product. What exact behavior changes, and why must it be VR instead of a phone video or a normal drill?

**Yonathan:** Because the entire point is to feel the earthquake. A normal drill is predictable, so students do not take it seriously.

**Model:** Feeling intensity may improve presence, but presence is not the same as safer behavior. You are assuming the most expensive layer is the causal one. What evidence would falsify that?

**Yonathan:** If a simple phone simulation produced the same improvement in decisions, then headsets would not be necessary for the first version. But a video would still be too passive.

**Model:** Good distinction. Then your load-bearing feature is branching practice with measurement, not a headset. What can web technology honestly do today?

**Yonathan:** A browser can render a 3D room, present choices, time decisions, and change the next scenario. WebXR can add headset mode on supported devices. Voice can be another input.

**Model:** You just bundled three technologies. Do they multiply each other or merely decorate the demo?

**Yonathan:** They can multiply if the 3D scene creates spatial context, the adaptive layer changes difficulty from the decision, and voice lets the user respond without looking away from the scene. If voice fails, touch remains available.

**Model:** You called the adaptive layer ML. What model will you train, on what data, with what target?

**Yonathan:** I do not have a valid dataset. Calling it ML would be dishonest. I should use an explainable rules engine now and document that a learned policy would require validated behavioral data later.

**Model:** That is technology honesty. Now attack the Mexican version. Who owns a headset, who facilitates it, and what happens outside a wealthy school?

**Yonathan:** That is the biggest weakness. A headset-first launch creates a hardware and operations bottleneck. INEGI's latest data shows cellphones are far more common than household computers. I should make the first slice phone-first and treat WebXR as progressive enhancement.

**Model:** Name real global attempts. "VR exists" is not a benchmark.

**Yonathan:** Tokyo's 4DOH uses multi-sensory 3D disaster experiences. RoT Studio has adult MR and child VR earthquake modes. DHS has evaluated VR training systems for first responders as an actual procurement category.

**Model:** What do they get right, and what breaks at the border?

**Yonathan:** 4DOH proves experience can be richer than a screen, but the facility model does not scale to families. RoT gets the before-during-after sequence and age differentiation right, but Mexico needs Spanish, local housing, Civil Protection review, and lower-cost delivery. DHS proves the category is operational, but first-responder hardware and procurement do not translate to a household.

**Model:** Choose one vacuum. Compliance has a payer. Family rehearsal has reach but no obvious buyer. Measurement could become a horizontal layer. Which one survives your role?

**Yonathan:** Family rehearsal. It is the fastest way to prove whether the technology changes a decision. I will not solve the payer this week. I will build one apartment and three decisions.

**Model:** Why not measurement as the primary vacuum?

**Yonathan:** Because measurement without a concrete user experience becomes a dashboard with no behavior to measure. The family scenario creates the event; measurement is built into it.

**Model:** What harm from the chapter could your product repeat?

**Yonathan:** It could use fear, grief, or realistic injury to drive engagement, then confuse emotional reaction with learning.

**Model:** "Be ethical" is not a design decision.

**Yonathan:** Then the rule is minimum necessary intensity: no injuries, no dead-person likeness, a visible exit, a pause control, and only three scenarios. Measure choices and delay, not distress.

**Model:** Take a position on the provocation. Does one honest VR school save more lives than ten million people in a national drill?

**Yonathan:** I agree the drill can become theater, but the lives-saved comparison is not evidenced. My defensible claim is smaller: one adaptive rehearsal can produce more useful behavioral evidence than one routine drill that measures only participation and evacuation time.

**Model:** What would kill your idea?

**Yonathan:** If users complete the scenario but repeat the same unsafe choice after feedback, the adaptation is not training. If the 3D layer makes phones slow or excludes low-cost devices, the technical approach fails its Mexican translation. And if Civil Protection reviewers say the scenarios teach unsafe actions, it cannot launch.

**Model:** Final opening sentence for your team.

**Yonathan:** Do not buy headsets to make drills feel modern. First prove that three phone-based, measurable decisions improve one family's second attempt, then earn the right to add immersion.
