## Guion DEMO - 3 minutos y 30 segundos

### 0:00-0:25 - problema y usuario

> This is 90 Segundos. It is a phone-first earthquake rehearsal for a Mexican family that has attended drills but has never practiced difficult decisions at home. It does not predict earthquakes or certify readiness. It only measures choices, hesitation, and improvement inside this simulation.

### 0:25-0:55 - Dragon Stack

> The first layer is a real-time 3D apartment built with Three.js. The second is explainable adaptive logic: safe answers reduce cues and time, while unsafe answers add guidance. The third is optional Spanish voice input. Voice is detected by the browser and touch always remains available.

### 0:55-2:15 - recorrido

> I choose the household with an older family member, so the rehearsal adds a support cue. The first scenario begins with strong movement. I select protection under a firm table. Because the choice is safe in this scene, the next round becomes harder and gives me less time. In the second scenario I smell gas. I evacuate without using switches or the elevator. The final scenario tests the family meeting plan when the network is saturated.

### 2:15-2:50 - debrief y limites

> The debrief shows only what happened in these three decisions. It does not turn the result into a universal score and does not claim I will behave the same way in a real emergency. That evidence boundary is part of the product, not a disclaimer added at the end.

### 2:50-3:20 - prueba, bug y correccion

> Ten mechanical tests passed. Testing found a race condition: a late voice result could answer a hidden next scenario after the timer expired. I stopped voice on timeout and blocked answers when the simulation is not active. The persona test also found that the household profile looked decorative, so I connected it to a real support cue and redeployed.

### 3:20-3:50 - reflexion de 30 segundos

> What changed my mind this week is that I entered thinking immersion meant headsets. The evidence pushed me toward a phone-first product. The valuable technology is not maximum realism; it is a measurable practice loop that reaches the user now. A headset can come later, after the behavior change is proven.

## Guion FUTURE BENDING - 2 minutos

> What changed my mind this week is my definition of immersion. I started with the idea that a serious earthquake rehearsal had to begin with a VR headset. After comparing Tokyo's 4DOH, earthquake VR products, and Mexico's device reality, I changed my position. The first useful layer is not expensive hardware. It is a branching decision loop that makes a person act, records hesitation, and changes the next attempt. That is why I built 90 Segundos phone-first and kept WebXR as an upgrade path.
>
> I also changed my view of adaptive AI. I first wanted to call the difficulty system machine learning because that sounded stronger. But we do not have a validated dataset connecting simulator behavior to real outcomes. The honest version is a visible rules engine. A safe decision reduces help and time. An unsafe one adds a cue. It adapts practice without pretending to predict survival.
>
> My timing verdict is that Chapter 5 is ahead of schedule in technical capability but behind schedule in trustworthy deployment. Real-time 3D, browser voice, and WebXR already exist. The binding constraint I saw is not rendering. It is validated local content: Mexican housing, Civil Protection review, accessibility, and evidence that rehearsal improves behavior outside the screen. The technology can manufacture an experience today. We have not yet earned the right to call that experience readiness.
